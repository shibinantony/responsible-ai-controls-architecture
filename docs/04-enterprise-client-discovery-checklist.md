# Enterprise Client Discovery Checklist

Client Discovery Checklist

A reusable interview checklist for assessing Responsible AI control maturity without drifting into broad governance theory.

Interview discipline

Use the pattern: risk classification -> execution boundary -> control -> evidence -> monitoring. Stay in controls, workflows, ownership, telemetry, and Day 2 routines.

1. Use-Case Scope

Which AI types are in scope: traditional ML, GenAI, copilots, RAG, agentic workflows, vendor AI, or all of these?

Which business areas are highest priority: customer service, adviser support, claims, underwriting, operations, compliance, knowledge workflows, or product distribution?

Which use cases are already live, in pilot, or under approval?

2. Risk Classification

Do you already have an AI use-case risk-tiering model?

What is the consequence if the AI is wrong, incomplete, biased, or over-trusted?

Which use cases involve customers, financial outcomes, regulated advice, sensitive data, or automated actions?

3. Execution Boundaries

What can the AI decide, recommend, draft, trigger, or automate?

Where is human review mandatory today, and where should it be mandatory?

What are the approval, escalation, and override paths?

4. Control Design

What controls exist for input quality, grounding, data sensitivity, hallucination, bias, unsafe output, and policy violations?

Are controls embedded at runtime, review time, release time, or only in documentation?

How are vendor AI capabilities assessed and controlled?

5. Evidence And Auditability

What evidence is captured: prompt, response, sources, model version, user, approval, override, exception, and final action?

Can an AI-assisted outcome be reconstructed later?

Who reviews evidence, and how long is it retained?

6. Day 2 Monitoring

What signals are monitored after deployment: drift, quality degradation, unsafe outputs, control bypass, data leakage, user overreliance, incidents, or vendor changes?

Who owns monitoring, thresholds, triage, and remediation?

How are lessons from incidents fed back into controls?

7. Success Definition

For a part-time or focused advisory model, what would success look like after the first 4-6 weeks?

Which one or two high-impact use cases should be used to prove the operating model?

What existing governance forums, engineering teams, and risk owners must be aligned?
